# Optical correspondence: pyramid and rainbow angles

## Geometry shown in the scene

The model distinguishes two pyramid sections:

1. **Corner edge:** apex to a corner of the square base, inclined by **41.985759°** above horizontal (11:7).
2. **Face apothem:** apex to the midpoint of a base side, inclined by **51.842773°** above horizontal (11:7).

Two schematic spectra:

- a **primary** rainbow near the approximately **42°** angular radius;
- a separated, reversed-colour **secondary** rainbow, with a green reference near **51.83°**.

Coordinate contract of the web scene:

- **Z** is vertical in the user’s Fusion drawings; in the web scene **Y** is up and the base lies in **XZ**.
- Both rainbows are **vertical semicircles** in planes parallel to the median plane through the apex.
- The primary spectrum is centred on the pyramid. Its outer red display radius equals `PyramidHeight`, so the real (non-extended) corner edge ends at the apex where the spectrum passes.
- The secondary green display radius is `2 × PyramidHeight`; the real face apothem is shown on the front face and only its continuation beyond the apex is extended until it reaches the green band.

Display radii make the relationship legible. They are **not** the physical distance to a rainbow.

## Numerical comparison (11:7)

```text
Pyramid face slope        = 51.842773°
Secondary green reference = 51.830000°
Absolute difference       =  0.012773°

Complementary pyramid angle = 180° − 51.842773°
                            = 128.157227°
Reference scattering angle  ≈ 128.17°
```

In correspondence dated 1 April 2017, rainbow-optics specialist Philip Laven provided a wavelength-dependent secondary-rainbow calculation that made a quantitatively stated comparison possible (green near 525 nm, `n ≈ 1.33659`).

## What this does not claim

- that the pyramid creates or physically controls a rainbow;
- that equal-looking angles are the same physical quantity;
- that the builders intentionally encoded a 525 nm wavelength;
- that the correspondence proves a historical design programme.

It claims something narrower and reproducible: with the stated assumptions, the two numerical angles differ by 0.012773°, and the laboratory can display that relationship transparently.
